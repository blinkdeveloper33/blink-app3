
1. Executive Summary
Rise Digital Financial Corp (“Rise”) is a fintech-focused software company headquartered in Miami, Florida, with a mission to democratize short-term liquidity and simplify financial transactions for everyday consumers. Through our flagship product, Blink, we aim to transform how individuals access small amounts of money—namely $150–$300—quickly and transparently. Blink was born out of the recognition that traditional banking and credit products often prove inaccessible, slow, and overly complex for many who need immediate funds for minor emergencies or urgent expenses.
In Florida alone, we see a growing digital lending market projected to expand from $5.2 billion in 2021 to $18.1 billion by 2030. Despite this strong demand, payday lenders still capture a large swath of consumers because of convenient albeit exploitative structures (e.g., extremely high APRs and fees). Moreover, traditional loans or credit cards require significant paperwork, credit checks, and can result in long-term debt. Blink tackles this issue by offering short-term advances with flat fees, a user-friendly application, and a strict one-advance-at-a-time model to prevent debt cycles.
Rise’s operational strategy revolves around consumer-centric design, data-driven underwriting, and leveraging Open Banking (through providers like Plaid) to quickly assess user eligibility. We partner with FV Bank to hold liquidity and handle disbursements. Through this synergy, Rise seeks to empower consumers, especially in Florida, by providing immediate, transparent financial support that fosters responsible borrowing and avoids the pitfalls of predatory lending.

3. Problem Statement & Rationale
Accessing quick funds for emergency expenses remains a major challenge for many Americans. Traditional financial products, such as credit cards or personal loans, frequently involve credit checks, lengthy applications, or stringent requirements. This creates unnecessary friction for users who only need a small sum (often under $300) for urgent purposes—like covering a minor car repair, an unexpected medical co-pay, or avoiding bank overdraft charges.
In Florida specifically, payday lending has exploded into a $2.5 billion-plus industry, with average APRs hovering around 278%. Many borrowers fall into a debt cycle, where approximately 83% of payday loan recipients end up re-borrowing seven or more times. Meanwhile, those who do not qualify for traditional banking products often resort to informal sources: borrowing from friends or family, depleting any savings cushion they might have, or simply going without, which can lead to deeper financial instability.
The central problem is a lack of transparent, user-friendly, short-term liquidity solutions. Consumers with pressing needs either face exploitative fees and compounding interest (in the payday lending space) or unwieldy bureaucratic hurdles (in the traditional banking world). There is a vacuum for a product that sits between these two extremes—a product that respects the user, charges fair flat fees, and fosters responsible borrowing. Rise’s Blink solution aims to address exactly that gap by streamlining the borrowing process, encouraging financial literacy, and minimizing the risk of debt dependency.

4. Blink Product Description
Blink is a mobile application that offers short-term cash advances ranging from $150 to $300. The platform’s architecture is designed around four key principles:
Simplicity: Users download the Blink app, connect their bank account via a secure Open Banking integration (e.g., Plaid), and receive a decision in seconds regarding eligibility. No deep credit check is performed, avoiding the negative impact on credit scores.


Speed: Qualified users can opt for Instant or Standard disbursement. Instant disbursements typically take minutes and require a higher flat fee (e.g., $11.99), while Standard disbursements arrive in 1–3 business days for a lower flat fee (e.g., $5.99).


Transparency: Blink clearly displays the flat fee before the user confirms the advance. There are no hidden charges, no rolling interest, and no continuous re-borrowing traps. Users are limited to one active advance at a time, significantly reducing the chance of “loan stacking.”


Responsibility: In alignment with Rise’s focus on financial wellness, Blink includes budgeting tools, spending insights, and push notifications to remind users about upcoming repayment dates. Repayment is automatically debited on the user-selected date (within 31 days), helping to prevent missed payments and subsequent late charges or overdrafts.


On the back end, Blink integrates with FV Bank to house the liquidity pool that powers these short-term advances. Repayments and fees flow back into the FV Bank account, replenishing liquidity. This arrangement creates an efficient cycle of capital: funds go out to cover user emergencies and come back when the user repays, alongside the flat fee.
Overall, Blink stands out by fusing immediate relief for cash emergencies with a structured approach that discourages users from drifting into endless debt. It’s a nimble, tech-based alternative that meets real-world consumer needs while upholding ethical lending standards.

Our average user may be employed (receiving regular paychecks), but occasionally faces cash flow mismatches—like a gig economy worker, a retail employee, or a freelancer with sporadic invoicing. By addressing this broader demographic, Blink taps into a significant customer pool hungry for safe, transparent, and immediate financial tools.

6. Competitive Landscape
Blink operates at the intersection of traditional lending, payday loan companies, and earned wage access providers. Key competitor segments include:
Payday Lenders: These storefronts and online services typically charge high APRs and allow users to roll over loans, often resulting in ballooning debt. Blink differentiates itself by capping fees, restricting the amount borrowed, and prohibiting multiple concurrent advances.


Credit Cards & Bank Overdrafts: Some users rely on bank overdrafts or credit cards for urgent funds, but fees can be high and credit approvals or interest rates can be punishing. Blink’s flat-fee model remains simpler and more transparent.


Earned Wage Access Apps: Providers like Earnin or Dave offer small-sum pay advances often tied directly to earned wages. While they market themselves as “tip-based,” some users end up tipping amounts that effectively match or exceed typical loan interest rates. Blink stands apart via a fixed, predictable fee structure.


Personal Loan Platforms: Companies like SoFi or Upstart focus on larger amounts and require more detailed underwriting. Blink’s approach is highly targeted toward small, immediate advances that do not necessitate extensive credit checks.


Given this landscape, Blink’s speed, transparency, and flat-fee approach position it as a compelling alternative, especially for mid- to low-income consumers who require short-term liquidity without risk-laden terms.

8. Operational Plan & Technology
Rise’s operational plan for Blink hinges on efficiency, compliance, and user trust:
Banking Infrastructure: Funds for Blink’s liquidity are held in an account at FV Bank. This account is used to disburse advances and receive repayments.
Underwriting & Risk Assessment: Blink utilizes Open Banking APIs to quickly gather a user’s bank transaction data (via Plaid or a similar service). This near-real-time insight shapes our approval decisions, ensuring we only extend advances when there’s sufficient evidence the user can repay.
Automation & Decisioning: Our system automates the lion’s share of loan approvals, sending only edge cases (e.g., flagged transactions, unusual patterns) to manual review. This allows us to scale without inflating overhead costs.
Compliance & Legal: From the user’s perspective, Blink is a cash advance product with a flat fee. However, from a regulatory standpoint, small-dollar lending laws in each state can vary significantly. We maintain an active compliance program to ensure we meet or exceed local statutes, especially if we expand beyond Florida.
Scalability & Cloud Infrastructure: Blink is hosted on a secure, cloud-based platform that can handle spikes in demand. We deploy frequent updates to refine the app experience and to incorporate new compliance rules.
This operational approach lets us move swiftly while staying anchored to robust financial and regulatory best practices.


9. Risk Factors
Regulatory & Legal Risk


Small-dollar lending laws vary widely. Though Blink’s “cash advance” framing aims to avoid being classified strictly as a payday loan, regulators may scrutinize fees, terms, or repayment structures. We must maintain continuous legal monitoring.
Default & Credit Risk


Although amounts are modest ($150–$300), repeated defaults can accumulate into significant losses for Rise. We rely on tight underwriting standards (bank transaction analysis, thorough AML/KYC checks) to mitigate this risk.
Market Competition


The short-term lending segment is crowded, featuring payday lenders, credit card offerings, earned wage access apps, etc. Blink must differentiate via user experience, transparent fees, and responsible borrowing structures.
Overdraft & Negative User Experiences


Users who fail to maintain a sufficient balance can face bank fees or missed payments, leading to negative brand perception. Blink addresses this by sending friendly reminders and encouraging responsible usage.
Funding & Liquidity


If capital injections or lines of credit become constrained, Blink’s ability to fund new advances diminishes. Rise must manage liquidity carefully.
Maintaining a robust risk management framework, compliance team, and prudent underwriting helps contain these exposures and fortify user trust.

4. Legal Review
4.1. State-Level Regulatory Landscape (Florida)
Under Florida law, consumer finance and small-dollar loans are subject to Chapter 516 of the Florida Statutes, often capping interest rates or requiring licensing if the product is deemed a “loan.” Although Blink markets itself as a “cash advance,” there are still important considerations:
Licensure: Even if structured as a fee-based advance, regulators might interpret the product under existing loan statutes, especially if the user’s repayment is due on a specific date and the fee could be construed as interest. Rise must maintain a Florida Consumer Finance license if needed.
Fee Caps: Traditional payday loans in Florida cannot exceed a $500 principal and often have strict database reporting requirements. Blink’s $300 maximum is well below this cap, but the statutory environment around interest and finance charges can still apply.
Disclosure Requirements: Florida’s “Truth in Lending” provisions mandate clear, conspicuous fee disclosures. Blink’s approach—flat fees, displayed at the point of transaction—aims to meet or exceed those rules.
4.2. Federal Laws & Regulations
Truth in Lending Act (TILA): If classified as a loan, Blink may have to calculate and disclose an Annual Percentage Rate (APR), even if it’s a flat fee. Some states or the CFPB might consider the effective APR high if the fee is pegged to a short term.
Electronic Fund Transfer Act (EFTA) & Regulation E: Automatic debits from a user’s bank account must follow EFTA rules. Blink obtains the user’s explicit consent at the time of enrollment, ensuring compliance.
Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA): In the event of repayment issues, how Blink or a third-party handles collections must align with federal guidelines.
Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA) & Data Privacy: Handling user financial data triggers compliance with GLBA’s Safeguards Rule. Blink’s Privacy Policy must clearly outline data usage, sharing, and protection measures.
4.3. Interstate Expansion Considerations
Once Blink expands beyond Florida, each new jurisdiction might impose unique interest rate ceilings, licensing requirements, or disclosure rules. Potential future states (e.g., Georgia, Texas, California, New York) each have distinct guidelines. Rise must adopt a state-by-state approach, possibly registering for small-dollar lending licenses or forging partnerships with banks in those states.
